How ARZO makes money from this platform, and which of its two roles each capability serves. The
code already contains a SaaS fee engine. What it lacks is everything around the code: a licence
position, a payment processor that works in Qatar, and an ARZO deployment to sell from.

## The licence gate — R1, made concrete

### What the text says

`LICENCE:1-8` prepends this to the AGPL: retain the "Powered by Hi.Events" attribution "at the footer
of all web pages and emails generated by this software"; a modified version may rephrase it as
"Powered by [Your Company] based on Hi.Events", but "the link must always direct to
https://hi.events"; removing it requires a commercial licence. It cites AGPL §7(b) as its basis.

Three provisions decide what that means, and none of them is a legal conclusion this plan can draw:

| Provision | Text | Consequence for ARZO |
|---|---|---|
| §7(b) | permits "preservation of specified reasonable legal notices or author attributions in that material or in the Appropriate Legal Notices displayed by works containing it" (`LICENCE:361-363`) | Whether a footer on **every page and every email** fits 7(b) is contested. **Needs legal advice.** |
| §7, "further restrictions" | a non-permitted additional term is a further restriction, and "you may remove that term" (`LICENCE:377-381`) | The opposite reading. ARZO should not build a business on either. |
| §13 | a modified version "must prominently offer all users interacting with it remotely ... an opportunity to receive the Corresponding Source" (`LICENCE:541-552`) | Uncontested. Every subsystem ARZO builds in this codebase is Corresponding Source. |

Hi.Events' own page (hi.events/licensing, accessed 2026-09-29) says: "Hi.Events is open source under
AGPL with additional terms"; self-hosting is free "provided you keep the 'Powered by Hi.Events' link
visible on all public-facing pages and publish any modifications you make"; and "With a commercial
licence, yes — there is no disclosure obligation."

`CLA.md:11` grants Hi.Event Ltd. rights to sublicense contributions under AGPL **and a commercial
licence**. That is what makes a commercial licence for the whole upstream codebase possible at all.
Whether every contributor signed it is `UNVERIFIED`; the agreement should warrant it.

### The published tiers

As published on 2026-09-29 — confirm in the signed agreement, which is the contract, not the page:

| Tier | Published price | Scope, quoted |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | €499 / year + VAT | "one organization selling tickets for its own events on one domain"; "Removes the Powered by Hi.Events link" |
| Platform | €2,499 / year + VAT | "Unlimited organizations & client accounts", "Host events on behalf of clients", "Charge tenants fixed fees, percentages, or both"; covers "up to €1 million a year in tickets" — above that, contact licence@hi.events |

And the sentence that decides ARZO's tier: "If you host anyone else's events, even just two clients,
you need Platform."

### Decision: buy the Platform licence before the first public deployment

| Option | Obligations | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A. AGPL, no licence | Attribution on every page and email; publish the source of **every** in-tree subsystem — accreditation, access, offline sync — to every user | Only if ARZO is content to publish its work. It has not said so. |
| B. Standard | No attribution, no disclosure (per the page); own events, one domain | Fits only if ARZO never runs an event for a client |
| C. Platform | As B, plus third-party organizers and client events | **Recommended.** An events business runs clients' events. |
| D. Clean-room rewrite | — | Refused: `04` non-goal |

The reason is cost asymmetry: R1 is the one critical risk in `120` with a published price. The
trade-off is a permanent annual dependency. **Legal must confirm before signing:** whether rights to
in-tree proprietary work survive cancellation (the page says an installation "keeps working"; it does
not say the disclosure exemption survives); what "installation" and "domain" mean for staging,
on-site print hosts and native apps (`37`, `94`); how the €1 million is measured; whether later
upstream releases merged by ARZO are covered.

## The two roles, made concrete

| | ARZO as **operator** | ARZO as **vendor** |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Event client, attendees | Other organizers |
| Revenue | Service fees, production margin, ticket revenue | Per-ticket fees, subscriptions (`99`) |
| What wins | Depth, on-site reliability, trained-staff UX | Self-serve onboarding, breadth, support |
| Security bar | ARZO staff | MFA, pen test, hardened tenant isolation (`64`, `08`) |
| Payments | ARZO's own merchant account; ARZO is merchant of record | Split payments; the organizer is merchant of record |
| Licence tier | Platform if any client event, else Standard | Platform |

The conflicts are specific: a configurable on-site setup versus a simple self-serve one; internal
operations tooling that is worthless to a vendor customer; and data — cross-tenant benchmarking
(`55`) serves the vendor and exposes the operator's clients.

## Services versus licence revenue

**Services dominate, by construction, for at least the next several phases.** The on-site
capabilities that make ARZO different do not exist until Phases 2–4; the SaaS fee mechanism cannot
serve Qatar organizers (below); and no ARZO SaaS infrastructure exists. Software revenue in that
window is the per-ticket fee on ARZO's own and clients' ticketing.

So the model is **operator first, vendor by exception**: a capability is sold as software only after
it has run ARZO's own events and passes the gate below. This plan does not forecast revenue; no input
for one is verified.

## Payments: Stripe is not available to a Qatar business — potentially critical

`CONFIRMED` in code: SaaS fees exist only as Stripe application fees on direct charges.
Stripe's availability page (https://stripe.com/global, accessed 2026-09-29) lists the United Arab
Emirates but **not Qatar**, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait or Oman. Self-serve cross-border payouts are
limited to platforms in the US, UK, EEA, Canada or Switzerland paying connected accounts in those
regions (https://docs.stripe.com/connect/cross-border-payouts, accessed 2026-09-29).

| Consequence | Status |
|---|---|
| A Qatar-domiciled ARZO entity cannot open a Stripe account in its own name | Per the published list; `UNVERIFIED` with Stripe sales |
| A Qatar organizer cannot be a connected account taking card payments, so SaaS mode's fee path cannot serve ARZO's home market | Inference from the list plus the direct-charge flow; confirm with Stripe |
| With SaaS mode **off**, every payment on the installation lands in one Stripe account: ARZO is merchant of record for every organizer on it, and settles to them by hand | `StripePaymentIntentCreationService.php:122-125`; whether collecting for third parties is regulated in Qatar is `UNVERIFIED` — legal |

| Path | What it takes | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Contract through an entity in a Stripe country | Legal and tax structuring | Keeps the code path; Qatar organizers still cannot be connected accounts |
| A GCC provider with split or marketplace payments | Per-provider work (ARZ-191); provider `UNVERIFIED` | Fee collection rebuilt per provider |
| ARZO as merchant of record, fees netted at settlement | Legal review; settlement tooling not built | Natural for ARZO-run events; heavy for SaaS |
| Invoice platform fees monthly | A fee ledger (`99` PR2) | Works with any processor; ARZO carries credit risk |

**Recommendation:** for ARZO-run events, ARZO is merchant of record through whichever acquirer
finance selects. For external SaaS, choose the processor **before** any sale, because it decides
whether fees can be collected at all. This is `15`'s merchant-of-record question; escalate them together.

## Open questions

- **Does ARZO ever run an event on behalf of a client?** If yes, Platform tier. If ARZO sells only its own events on one domain, Standard suffices — confirm the reading with Hi.Events in writing.
- **Which legal entity contracts and takes payments?** Decides the processor and merchant of record. Business, legal and finance, together with `15`.
- **Is external SaaS a goal in the next twelve months?** Leaning no: operator first. It changes `100` and `101` from "design" to "defer".
- **Is ARZO's ticketing live anywhere today?** No deployment is evidenced. If one exists outside this repository, its licence and payment position must be audited first.
